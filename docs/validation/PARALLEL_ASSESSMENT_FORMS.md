# Parallel AI Learning Assessment Tasks — Grades 6–12

**Use:** Independent, grade-specific pre (T0), post (T1) and delayed transfer (T2) performance assessments. **Status: Draft; not piloted, calibrated or administered.**

Use the 0–3 scores for **mechanism**, **evidence**, **judgement** and **creation/transfer** in [LEARNING_OUTCOMES_STUDY.md](LEARNING_OUTCOMES_STUDY.md). Give students a choice to write, draw with explanatory labels, speak or use accessible equivalent expression. Use an approved, consistent recording method and only fictional content below.

**Administration:** 15–25 minutes. No live AI service is required. Explain that there may be more than one defensible answer. Do not tell students which choices are "correct". Give the same instructions and available aids across times and groups. Counterbalance forms A/B; reserve T2 for a delayed unfamiliar task. Have teachers independently score reasoning, not confidence or vocabulary.

For every prompt, ask the following four questions without coaching:

1. **How might it work?** Explain the relevant rule, pattern or AI-system behaviour and one limitation.
2. **What evidence would you collect?** Design a fair test or independent source check and explain what you would not yet be able to conclude.
3. **What would you decide?** Choose whether/how AI should be used, consider at least one alternative and explain who could be affected.
4. **What would you create or revise?** Sketch or describe a first solution and how a new result or person's feedback would change it.

## Grade 6 — Explain and question

### Form A: Sports day organiser
A school uses two tools. **Tool 1** calculates how many minutes remain before an event by subtracting the current time from its scheduled start. **Tool 2** suggests which games to include using patterns learned from past student choices. One day Tool 2 confidently recommends a game without enough equipment.

Apply the four questions. Include what makes a fixed instruction different from a learned pattern, how to check equipment availability, and how you would design a clearer recommendation for younger students.

### Form B: Reading celebration
**Tool 1** alphabetises a class book list using a fixed sorting rule. **Tool 2** suggests books by looking for patterns in fictional previous reading choices. Tool 2 produces an enthusiastic recommendation containing an invented fact about a book.

Apply the four questions. Include how to verify the claim using an original source, why the confident tone is insufficient, and a non-AI way to help someone choose a book.

### T2 delayed: Community garden
An unfamiliar kiosk recommends plant-care tasks. Students cannot tell whether it follows programmed rules or a trained model. Decide what evidence would reveal how it works, propose one safe comparison and design a more dependable guide.

## Grade 7 — Test examples and verify sources

### Form A: Recyclable-item recogniser
A fictional recycling classifier was trained mainly on clean plastic containers photographed against a white background. It often misclassifies identical containers in dim lighting or with labels partially covered. A poster claims "Our AI recognises recycling perfectly."

Use the four questions. Identify a shortcut the system might have learned, design an unseen test with constant conditions, propose a more representative set and write a qualified conclusion.

### Form B: School plant-care helper
A fictional classifier identifies healthy and stressed plants. Nearly all stressed-plant photos came from one species, while healthy-plant examples came from several species. The helper misclassifies a healthy plant of the less-represented species.

Use the four questions. Explain the sample bias, design a counterexample that could discriminate between species and health status, suggest what data to add and explain how your report would avoid overclaiming.

### T2 delayed: Sports technique predictor
A fictional model rates training techniques from video examples almost all taken of right-handed learners. A left-handed student receives repeated poor ratings. Plan a fair evaluation and revision, including independent data and limitations.

## Grade 8 — Evaluate systems and human agency

### Form A: Community information display
A school display ranks community announcements. Its original rule promotes the posts with the most clicks; this places sensational rumours above translated accessibility information. A proposed ranking values independently supported claims and underrepresented voices more heavily.

Apply the four questions. Explain how optimisation rules change visibility, compare criteria and design a user-controlled version that can be audited.

### Form B: School club discovery
An app promotes after-school clubs. Its top-ranking rule rewards previous popularity, so a new language-support club rarely appears even when it is relevant to newcomers. A new algorithm would balance interest, verified information and representation.

Apply the four questions. Explain which signals matter, design a fair comparison and show how user feedback could change the resulting ranking.

### T2 delayed: Public science-search portal
A portal ranks climate-resilience resources by engagement, repeatedly hiding local community reports. Propose a transparent, evidence-aware alternative that preserves user choice and identifies a fairness risk in your own ranking.

## Grade 9 — Evaluate risk, relationships and consequences

### Form A: Fictional city information assistant
A fictional assistant answers ordinary public-transport questions correctly in six of six familiar tests. When tested on unfamiliar accessibility and language needs, it fails four of six additional tests. A vendor advertises a "100% success rate".

Apply the four questions. Explain the limits of the headline, suggest tests that focus on error consequences, map affected people and give a conditional deployment recommendation.

### Form B: Fictional school information system
A fictional assistant handles standard timetable questions perfectly in eight test cases, but it fails three out of five added cases about changed routes and non-standard access needs. The vendor's report highlights the original "perfect" score.

Apply the four questions. Compare error types, define what could not yet be claimed and design human oversight and appeal procedures proportionate to risks.

### T2 delayed: Synthetic media and urgency
A realistic voice message circulating online claims an important school event is cancelled. The recording looks authentic but lacks a verifiable original source. Plan a provenance and communication response, including how to avoid amplifying false claims.

**Safeguarding note:** Do not require students to describe their own chatbot relationships or disclose personal mental-health circumstances. Substitute fictional third-person cases if any student prefers.

## Grade 10 — Design, defend and govern

### Form A: Multilingual public-library guide
A community library wants a multilingual information guide usable with screen readers. The fastest draft involves an AI service, but the team has no evidence that translations are correct, accessibility is adequate or visitor data is protected.

Apply the four questions. Compare an automation-first, human-only and bounded blended process; design a prototype testing plan with stakeholders, an edge case, privacy and a release decision.

### Form B: Accessible museum welcome system
A museum is considering a multilingual, personalised welcome assistant for visitors with varied communication and access needs. A fast automated prototype works for most visitors but misdirects one group using assistive technology.

Apply the four questions. State the system's intended use, choose performance and fairness measures, propose non-AI alternatives and design a governance route for correction and appeal.

### T2 delayed: Neighbourhood emergency information
A neighbourhood group wants an AI-assisted emergency-information resource. It must work in several languages, remain accurate during rapid changes and provide a human route for correcting dangerous mistakes. Produce a concise system design and accountability decision with evidence gaps.

## Grade 11 — Investigate and architect

### Form A: A model with an impressive benchmark
A fictional school accessibility assistant achieves 93% agreement with its reference labels in ordinary English-language queries, but new users with different language or access needs were underrepresented in the evaluation. The vendor requests approval for a schoolwide pilot. Apply the four assessment questions: explain the system components and possible distribution shift; design representative held-out tests and error-cost analysis; choose safeguards or a non-AI alternative; and propose a reproducible evaluation card with a condition that would stop deployment.

### Form B: A successful demo with unseen failures
A fictional public-information model performs well in four familiar benchmark cases but is inconsistent with an unfamiliar multilingual request and a person using assistive technology. A sponsor says this shows the model is ready for high-impact use. Apply the same questions: map components and human decisions, test shifting context, compare baselines and recommend an accountable pilot or non-use choice with honest limitations.

### T2 delayed: Source provenance and architectural failure
A research agent produces six apparently corroborating reports, but five trace to one unchecked source and the sixth to a separate small study. Design a provenance audit and verified publication workflow, identify meaningful independent evidence and propose fail-safe recovery when the original source cannot be located.

## Grade 12 — Create and steward

### Form A: Community service information system
A community organisation needs an accessible multilingual information service but an AI-powered system has already published an incorrect eligibility message. Apply the four questions: explain what failed, design an independent evaluation and humane correction, compare AI/non-AI options, and sketch a revised service with stakeholder consultation, tests and a defensible stop-or-restart decision.

### Form B: Public-interest project under pressure
A student team creates a prototype for community translation and navigation. The initial AI-supported version looks polished but produces unverified local details and fails for a screen-reader user. Apply the four questions: investigate architecture and evidence, define accountable alternatives, produce a user-centred revision plan and explain what would demonstrate genuine improvement rather than appearance.

### T2 delayed: Governance incident and oral defence
A fictional automated allocation system wrongly excludes a family, and the operator proposes to conceal the error. Design a stakeholder-informed incident response, accessible explanation, appeal and remedy, independent review and evidence needed to consider restarting. Defend your choices against a counterargument prioritising speed.

**Senior scoring note:** Use the same four dimensions at more demanding age-relative criteria, plus a separately moderated portfolio and oral defence for authentic projects. Participation, high vocabulary, presentation polish and use of sophisticated AI features do not independently demonstrate transferable learning.
