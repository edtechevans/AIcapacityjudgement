# Capacity × Judgement: Grade 11 and Grade 12

**Version:** v24 · **Curriculum:** Grades 6–12 · **Structure:** 42 modules, seven grade experiences, six strands, ten offline interactive laboratories.

**Status:** Original curricular design integrated into the public website for testing. It has **not** been formally evaluated by the IB, endorsed by the IB, or validated by student learning outcomes. It is a supplementary interdisciplinary resource rather than an official DP subject or an assessed Diploma Programme course.

## Why the senior years are different

The seven-year progression is:

| Grade | Developmental identity | Evidence expectation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | Explore & Question | Distinguish observation and prediction; verify an example |
| 7 | Apply & Verify | Compare conditions and check independent sources |
| 8 | Design & Decide | Prototype, evaluate ranking choices and consider user needs |
| 9 | Evaluate & Challenge | Investigate significant error modes and affected people |
| 10 | Transfer & Lead | Defend unfamiliar decisions, test prototypes and propose safeguards |
| **11** | **Investigate & Architect** | Produce reproducible system evaluations, research audits and independently defended designs |
| **12** | **Create & Steward** | Demonstrate accountable original creation, meaningful revision, public defence and justified stewardship |

Grades 11 and 12 still use **Learn → Model → Practise → Apply → Reflect**, but the intellectual focus shifts from completing structured tasks to independently designing evaluation methods, comparing alternatives, producing evidence and defending consequential choices.

## Grade 11 — Investigate & Architect (DP1)

**Essential question:** How can we design and investigate AI-supported systems without surrendering our own understanding or responsibility?

| Module | Independent learning | Core evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A1 Beyond the Model** | Explore system architecture, distribution shift, threshold effects and consequential errors | Model evaluation card and bounded trial/rejection |
| **A2 Designing Intelligent Workflows** | Architecture, permission boundaries, source verification and fail-safe recovery | Auditable workflow and controlled comparison |
| **A3 From Problem to System** | Stakeholder needs, prototyping, accessibility and testing | Independent design dossier and usability revision |
| **B1 The Architecture of Choice** | Automation bias, intellectual independence and principled non-use | Personal agency protocol and defended examples |
| **B2 Evidence, Knowledge & Uncertainty** | Source provenance, independence and research uncertainty | Provenance map and calibrated conclusion |
| **B3 Systems, Power & Fairness** | Distributional effects, stakeholder rights and accessible appeals | Operational equity and accountability review |

The **AI Systems Investigation Lab** in 11-A1 includes a fictional threshold model. Learners change score cutoffs and error severity, compare three held-out conditions and inspect false exclusions. The lab is not a real admissions system, fairness score or validated predictive model.

The **Evidence Provenance Lab** in 11-B2 compares repeated headlines with independent methods. Learners manipulate the counting rule, inspect traceable origins and decide what they can justifiably conclude.

**Senior project:** 11-A3 is the DP1 design dossier, normally three substantial studio sessions plus feedback. Strong work explains a stakeholder need, compares AI/non-AI alternatives, produces original work and documents a tested revision. 11-B3 is a complementary operational fairness inquiry.

## Grade 12 — Create & Steward (DP2)

**Essential question:** What responsibility do we have for the systems, decisions and consequences we help bring into the world?

| Module | Independent learning | Core evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A1 AI at the Frontier** | Assess agentic/multimodal claims, permissions and independent benchmarks | Future-technology evaluation brief |
| **A2 Orchestrate, Verify, Govern** | Design and stress-test tools, logs, permissions and recovery | Workflow stress-test and recovery record |
| **A3 The Impact Project** | Self-directed authentic inquiry, prototype, evidence, revision and defence | A complete accountable impact portfolio |
| **B1 Human Intelligence in an AI World** | Purpose, identity, careers, ownership and non-use | Personal charter and oral defence |
| **B2 Defending What We Know** | Counterarguments, method comparisons, uncertainty and reasoning | Evidence ledger, qualified argument and revision |
| **B3 Stewardship & Accountability** | Incident response, accessible redress, governance and correction | Tested governance charter and incident-response trace |

In **12-A2**, learners compare a fully autonomous workflow, a bounded verified design and a human-led baseline. They independently inject three plausible failures: missing evidence, tool outage and private-data risk. These are transparent fictional teaching cases.

In **12-B3**, learners rehearse organisational responses to a fictional harmful system decision. By changing an affected person's accessibility needs they evaluate whether human review and actual remedies are obtainable. Hiding error is included only as a case to critique, never as an acceptable option.

### Grade 12 signature experience: The Impact Project

The **Impact Project** is the culminating, independently defended portfolio. Plan for **six to eight weekly studio blocks plus a stakeholder or educator conference**, with appropriate flexibility for real classroom schedules.

1. **Discover**: Ethically identify a genuine need, stakeholders and consent boundaries.
2. **Investigate**: Compare sources and use/non-use alternatives; agree observable success criteria.
3. **Design**: Produce an original prototype, creative artefact, research product, policy or non-technical intervention.
4. **Evaluate**: Test usability, accessibility, factual accuracy, privacy, edge cases and impact claims.
5. **Revise**: Record observations, user feedback, changes and important unresolved limitations.
6. **Defend**: Present the evidence, AI contribution disclosure, limitations and accountability to a real or teacher-approved audience.

A carefully evidenced **decision not to use or deploy AI** is a valid and potentially exemplary outcome.

In the current public site, learners see project phases and can mark planning milestones in memory. These are **not verified mastery or an official submission**. Downloadable notes include investigation prompts and milestone indicators; real artefacts should be maintained in school-approved systems for teacher moderation.

## Designed for the IB Diploma context

These are **conceptual and skills connections**, not claims of formal IB alignment, accreditation or a replacement for existing requirements.

- **Theory of Knowledge:** knowledge claims, justification, interpretation, perspective, reliability and uncertainty. B2 modules support evaluating which evidence warrants a conclusion.
- **Extended Essay:** independent research, research question framing, evidence provenance, method, authentic authorship and responsible acknowledgement of AI use. Any specific AI permissions must follow the current school and subject assessment rules.
- **Creativity, Activity, Service:** real community needs, appropriately consented stakeholder work, meaningful reflection and service-minded design. An AI Impact Project is not automatically a CAS experience and must satisfy CAS guidance independently.
- **DP subjects:** authentic enquiries in sciences, language acquisition, individuals and societies, mathematics and design where appropriate, without claiming domain expertise from AI output.

Students joining AISG in Grade 11 can select Grade 11 directly. Each senior studio provides a **six-question grade-specific starting snapshot**, optional scaffolds and a concise foundation reminder. Students do **not** have to complete all earlier grades.

## Assessment and learning evidence

The same four human-reviewed dimensions apply, at higher expectations:

| Dimension | Grade 11 | Grade 12 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanism | Diagrams a system and defines limitations | Evaluates an unfamiliar system and predicts failure pathways |
| Evidence | Runs reproducible tests, compares sources and calibrates certainty | Independently defends a conclusion, welcomes counterevidence and revises |
| Judgement | Designs safeguards and justifies AI use or non-use | Demonstrates stewardship, authority, affected-person participation and redress |
| Creation and transfer | Tests an original prototype or evaluation in a new context | Presents an independently reviewed, revised impact portfolio for an authentic audience |

The public website checks short guided questions and records self-reported practice. Only teachers, qualified audiences and appropriate evidence can determine the quality of original research and capstones.

## Inclusive and safe facilitation

- No live AI service or personal account is required. Labs use fictional datasets, deterministic mechanisms and explicit limits.
- Offer paper-based source networks, printed threshold cards, role-based workflow diagrams, accessible text/table formats, bilingual language support and oral alternatives.
- Do not collect private student, caregiver, admissions or learning-support records in this GitHub demo or in an unapproved model.
- Constrain authentic stakeholder contact to consented and school-approved procedures; use a teacher-provided persona when needed.
- Design for accessibility, language inclusion and purposeful AI **non-use**.
- Review current school guidance for transparency, acceptable AI assistance, subject integrity and safeguarding before running a real Diploma project.
