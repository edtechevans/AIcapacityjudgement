# Educator pathway V3 — Capacity × Judgement (TLF / MTSS integrated)

**Status:** Curriculum design specification; not a live-site deployment.
**Source:** AISG Transformative Learning Framework Learning Engagement Indicators (38 official indicators), Faculty Field Guide, What Guides Us questions, Quick Reference, and Showcase Deck.
**Architecture:** Two equal strands, four modules each. Each module targets 30 active learning minutes. Total 4 hours (2 hours Capacity, 2 hours Judgement).
**Audience:** MYP/DP and wider secondary educators, transferable to other school contexts.

## Central promise
AI can expand what educators can do. Professional judgement determines whether that additional capacity deepens learning, strengthens learner agency, removes barriers, and produces meaningful action.

The TLF remains the pedagogical DNA, not an extra compliance curriculum. The official six facets are Personalisation, Agency, Authenticity, Creativity, Taking Action, Collaboration. The paired dimensions are Being (Personalisation and Agency), Connecting (Authenticity and Creativity), Doing (Collaboration and Taking Action), centred on Engagement for All. TLF indicators are lenses rather than a checklist.

### Boundaries
- Keep all existing 42 student modules unchanged.
- All learner interactions are selected-response: single-choice, multi-select, classify, sequence with accessible buttons, diagnose/revise by selecting alternatives, and scenario branches. No text entry, upload, written reflection, speech, or drag-only interaction.
- Ensure challenge, feedback and transfer are different situations. Discriminating distractors must be plausible.
- Separate completion of checkpoints from validated professional competence.
- Use fabricated, de-identified student cases; never prompt users to paste identifiable student records into AI.
- MTSS is an educational decision cycle, **not** an automated diagnosis: universal quality instruction (Tier 1), targeted additional support (Tier 2), intensive individualized support (Tier 3), evidence review, fidelity and responsiveness. Do not infer placement from one score or use AI to make special education determinations.
- Maintain the six V2 product quality criteria: learning impact, intellectual engagement, instructional quality, accessibility, trustworthiness and evidence.

## Strand A — CAPACITY: Understand what AI makes possible
*Do not present capability as guaranteed reliability or automatic instructional improvement.*

### C1 | Understand the Capacity: What AI Can—and Cannot—Do (30 minutes)
**Question:** What kind of work can an AI system meaningfully support, and what evidence should temper confidence?
**Learning:** Generative/predictive AI, training and inference, probabilistic output, contexts/limitations, hallucinations, provenance, data input boundaries, appropriate non-use.
**TLF:** Authenticity 3.4 (inquiry, research and information literacy); Agency 2.3 (reflection on thinking).
**Learning experiences:** compare accurate-looking unsupported outputs; classify six tasks as useful assistance, verify independently, or do not use; sequence a verification workflow; transfer to a fabricated school-policy summary.
**Checkpoint:** Select a defensible claim about model capability and identify a failure mode in an unfamiliar context.

### C2 | Personalisation for All: Removing Barriers, Maintaining Challenge (30 minutes)
**Question:** How can AI broaden access without doing the thinking for students?
**Learning:** UDL-aligned multiple means of access and expression; linguistic/cultural accessibility; cognitive load; accessibility; language support; differentiation that preserves common ambitious goals.
**TLF:** Personalisation 1.1, 1.4, 1.6; Agency 2.2.
**MTSS:** Tier 1 universal design first; use assessment and response evidence to consider additional targeted supports, not label learners.
**Learning experiences:** match learner barriers to scaffolds (audio, vocabulary, language pathways, worked examples, graduated cues); reject answer-giving shortcuts; classify universally available versus targeted supports; transfer to a mixed-proficiency MYP inquiry.
**Checkpoint:** Choose supports that widen access while keeping target reasoning visible.

### C3 | Creativity and Authenticity: Designing Beyond the Prompt (30 minutes)
**Question:** When does AI help students investigate, create and contribute rather than just produce?
**Learning:** divergent ideation, concept criticism, authentic audiences, inquiry, original contribution, information literacy, transfer, cross-disciplinary learning.
**TLF:** Creativity 4.1–4.3; Authenticity 3.1, 3.4, 3.7; Taking Action 5.2.
**Learning experiences:** distinguish polished output from student originality; compare a worksheet automation with a community-facing inquiry; sequence a design cycle where human decisions and verification remain explicit; transfer to DP science or humanities investigation.
**Checkpoint:** Identify the design with the strongest authentic purpose, intellectual demand and student ownership.

### C4 | Collaborative Capacity: Better Workflows, Shared Learning (30 minutes)
**Question:** How can AI save educator time and strengthen collaborative learning without diluting relationships?
**Learning:** safe educator workflows, lesson co-planning, resource adaptation, drafting versus checking, collective critique, feedback co-design, iterative improvement.
**TLF:** Collaboration 6.1, 6.3, 6.4, 6.6; Doing dimension.
**MTSS:** collaborative problem solving using evidence; teacher teams identify hypotheses, interventions, measures and review dates; retain human decisions.
**Learning experiences:** categorize AI-supported workflow steps and human-owned steps; troubleshoot flawed group tasks; sequence plan→review→adapt→check; transfer to team planning an inclusive unit.
**Checkpoint:** Build an accountable workflow preserving privacy, dialogue, equitable contribution and teacher responsibility.

## Strand B — JUDGEMENT: Decide when capacity improves learning
*Every module requires a defensible reason to use, limit or reject AI.*

### J1 | Agency and Intellectual Ownership: Who Does the Thinking? (30 minutes)
**Question:** Which intellectual work must remain with the learner?
**Learning:** productive struggle, metacognition, authentic student voice and choice, transparent assistance, substitution vs scaffold, age/developmental appropriateness.
**TLF:** Agency 2.1–2.3, 2.6, 2.9; Personalisation 1.3.
**Learning experiences:** compare three AI-use policies for a writing/inquiry task; spot where thinking disappears; classify student/AI/shared responsibilities; transfer to an unfamiliar subject task.
**Checkpoint:** Defend the option that protects target thinking and meaningful agency, with feedback on trade-offs.

### J2 | Evidence Before Intervention: MTSS, Inclusion and Belonging (30 minutes)
**Question:** When learners struggle, what evidence justifies the next support?
**Learning:** multiple converging data sources, universal access and fidelity first, frequent formative checks, response-to-intervention monitoring, avoiding deficit language and bias; observe patterns rather than diagnose.
**TLF:** Personalisation 1.1, 1.5–1.6; Agency 2.3; Collaboration 6.6; belonging/cultural responsiveness and SEL.
**MTSS:** tiered practice cycle; Tier 1 high-quality universal instruction; additional targeted supports where indicated; intensified individual planning with professional teams; progress monitoring and review.
**Learning experiences:** distinguish signal from stereotype in fictional multilingual learner cases; sequence evidence→barrier hypothesis→support→monitor→adjust; choose among universal design, targeted follow-up, or consultation; transfer using conflicting attendance/engagement/academic signals.
**Checkpoint:** Select a proportionate response, explain what additional evidence is needed, and reject automated tier assignment.

### J3 | Assessment, Feedback and Trust: What Can We Actually Know? (30 minutes)
**Question:** How can an educator make credible claims about learning when AI contributes?
**Learning:** validity, authenticity of evidence, process checkpoints, oral/independent verification without requiring course learners to speak, feedback that fosters agency, privacy, fairness, errors and uncertainty.
**TLF:** Authenticity 3.4; Agency 2.3; Creativity 4.2; Collaboration 6.4.
**Learning experiences:** classify evidence as strong/weak for a learning claim; compare feedback that revises for the student vs feedback that helps them revise; decide on assessment redesign; transfer to unfamiliar DP/MYP task.
**Checkpoint:** Select a valid assessment and safeguard package, without treating a polished artifact as proof of understanding.

### J4 | From Judgement to Taking Action: A Transformative Learning Decision Lab (30 minutes)
**Question:** What should we change, preserve or refuse—and how will we know whether the change helped students?
**Learning:** cumulative synthesis: identify target learning, assess AI capability, anticipate harms, design inclusive access, protect thinking, connect authentic purpose, choose evidence, review and iterate.
**TLF:** Taking Action 5.1, 5.2, 5.4–5.5; integrates Being/Connecting/Doing and the other five facets.
**MTSS:** evidence-informed, appropriately tiered supports and review cycles without automated labeling.
**Learning experiences:** interactive six-step case with 2–3 decisions at each step; choose non-use when appropriate; compare immediate efficiency to longer-term learning; transfer to a new subject/age/context without copying the worked example.
**Checkpoint:** Final cumulative branching simulation: design→notice→reflect→improve using student talk, choices, work, relationships and action as evidence. Award completion only after each critical safeguard is addressed.

## Consistent 30-minute module learning rhythm
- 5 min: Learn — explicit principle, misconception and quick understanding check
- 5 min: Model — contrast stronger and weaker professional approaches
- 8 min: Practise — varied selected-response decisions with explanatory feedback
- 8 min: Apply — authentic educational case demanding a defensible choice
- 4 min: Transfer — unfamiliar context and concise takeaway
Time guidance is flexible, not a timer or a reading-speed requirement. Prioritise intellectual demand over content volume, and reserve the final decision lab for a compressed but cumulative branching case.

## Assessment patterns
At least four distinct interaction families per module across the sequence, with deliberate distribution rather than one repetitive template. Use meaningful single-choice only when a clearly strongest option exists; multi-select where safeguards jointly matter; classification for evidence/scope/roles; button-driven ordering for decision cycles; branching simulations for cumulative judgement. Feedback should consistently explain **what you noticed → what matters → try this elsewhere**.

Sample item (J2): A multilingual student performs poorly on one timed English-language task, but can explain the concept accurately using visuals. The next step is not an automated Tier 2 referral. Instead examine whether the assessment introduced an unnecessary language barrier, provide Tier 1 access pathways, and collect further evidence of the target concept. The correct response must preserve rigorous learning goals.

## Implementation acceptance criteria
1. Exactly four Capacity and four Judgement modules in Educators UI, both with equal prominence, each visibly labelled 30 minutes.
2. Eight modules contain original explicit teaching, contrasting examples, professional decisions, differentiated feedback, application and unfamiliar transfer; not just 8 renamed old cards.
3. All no-written-answer and accessibility safeguards remain intact.
4. End-to-end progress works when moving from 12 old module IDs to 8 new ones; do not silently grant completion of newly substantive content using earlier progress keys.
5. Confirm student modules, sitewide UI/branding, and all student routes remain unchanged.
6. Confirm tests and browser QA before publicly claiming deployment.
7. Keep the TLF attribution explicit in course introduction/credits but mostly implicit in learning tasks, and clarify that MTSS concepts are adaptations, not official AISG TLF indicators.
