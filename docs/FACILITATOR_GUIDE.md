# Facilitator Guide — From Learning Studio to Authentic Classroom Learning
**Grades 6–10 · AI Capacity × Judgement**

The Learning Studio teaches ideas, provides worked examples, offers guided practice and prepares students to apply what they know. It does **not** replace feedback from teachers, peers, real users or trustworthy sources.

## A practical lesson structure

**Before (5 minutes):** Choose a relevant subject challenge and identify the learning goal. Decide what students should do independently before considering AI support. If using AI, confirm tool approval, data protection and classroom expectations.

**During (15–25 minutes):** Students explore the relevant AI Capacity and AI Judgement modules. Ask learners to explain why the stronger worked example is stronger. Prompt learners to check claims independently and decide whether AI belongs in the task.

**Beyond the screen (variable):** Students create a real artefact or demonstration—prototype, argument, annotated comparison, guide, poster, oral defence, experiment or storyboard. Invite a peer/user to give feedback and have students revise. A complete module checkpoint means the learner *reported practice*, not that the teacher assessed the quality.

**Reflection (5–10 minutes):** Students explain what changed in their understanding or approach, cite specific evidence and propose a transfer to a new task. Written, spoken and visual evidence can all be valid.

## A simple formative look-for rubric

Use these as observations, not as labels of students. Gather evidence from the product, explanation, decision process and response to feedback.

| Lens | What to look for | Question to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Understanding** | Explains how the relevant AI mechanism or limitation works, accurately and in their own words | "What did you believe before? How would you explain this to a younger student?" |
| **Judgement and agency** | Justifies AI use, limited use or non-use against the actual learning goal | "Which thinking did you keep, and why?" |
| **Evidence and feedback** | Verifies claims, uses credible sources, responds to critique and can explain a change | "What evidence made you trust or revise that decision?" |
| **Transfer and action** | Applies the idea beyond the worked example to an authentic audience or problem | "What would you do differently next time, and what might not transfer?" |

### Interpreting formative evidence

- **Needs more modelling:** A student can repeat terms but cannot yet explain, test or justify them.
- **Developing with support:** The student makes a reasonable choice or correction when given prompts, examples or feedback.
- **Independent application:** The student explains the decision and produces evidence without step-by-step help.
- **Transfer to a new context:** The student adapts the approach, recognises limitations and defends choices in a different task.

Do not use a single automated multiple-choice check or one self-confirmed checkbox as sufficient proof of mastery. Those checks are invitations to practise and seek feedback.

## Examples of meaningful subject integration

- **G6 Science:** Compare a timer and a recogniser, verify a claim, and teach the difference to a younger student.
- **G7 Language & Literature:** Revise an original persuasive paragraph using feedback, preserving the author's purpose.
- **G8 Design:** Prototype an inclusive community resource, test with users, and justify AI/non-AI workflow choices.
- **G9 Individuals & Societies:** Evaluate synthetic messaging, provenance and underrepresented perspectives.
- **G10 Personal Project / Interdisciplinary:** Defend the decision record, demonstrate evidence of testing, consult stakeholders and describe responsible safeguards.

## Learning safety and agency

- Use only school-approved tools, age-appropriate policies and teacher-supplied materials where needed.
- No student is required to give data to an external AI service to complete a Learning Studio module.
- Avoid entering classmates' identities, sensitive information or unconsented images.
- In many tasks, deciding not to use AI is evidence of sound judgement, not lack of capability.
- Encourage verification through class materials, original sources and human feedback.
- The studio uses a **local display alias**, not verified student authentication. Progress is device-local and written artefacts remain in the current browser tab unless exported.

## Student pilot to validate the learning itself

Observe two students per grade across G6–G10. For each, record whether they independently (1) understand a new idea, (2) use feedback to improve a decision, (3) make an authentic product or argument, (4) explain why they chose AI or non-use, and (5) transfer that reasoning to an unfamiliar task. Ask which part of the teaching *actually changed their thinking*, rather than only whether they liked the website.
