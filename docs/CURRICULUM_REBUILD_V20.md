# Capacity × Judgement: Curriculum Rebuild v20

**Grades:** 6–10 · **Design:** 30 modules, six strands, five learning stages · **Status:** Original authored content and testable software changes; **not** externally endorsed or validated by student outcomes.

## The instructional change

This release keeps the dual commitment: **Capacity** (understand, work with and create using AI) and **Judgement** (purpose/agency, truth/trust, ethics/consequences). The emphasis changes from reading and choosing answers to **investigating a problem, gathering evidence, making a reasoned decision, creating or testing a response, revising it, and transferring the learning**.

Every module now contains an original, age-appropriate investigation with:
- a compelling question that admits more than a one-word answer;
- an observable procedure that can be completed with teacher-provided materials, accessible alternatives or approved technology;
- a specific artefact or body of evidence to present;
- a transfer challenge beyond the original activity.

For Grades 6–8, tasks build from distinguishing tools, reading data, controlled comparison and human-centred design to investigations of ranking, voice and fairness. For Grades 9–10, tasks become evaluations of reliability, emotional influence, workplace systems, uncertainty, governance, and independently defended design.

**No learner has to sign into a public generative-AI system.** All new in-site activities use teacher-authored, fictional, offline examples. Teachers can extend them with approved tools and real research in their own classrooms.

## Five deep-dive laboratory experiences

| Grade / module | What the learner manipulates | What changes visibly | Core learning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **G7 A1**: How do models learn? | Skewed vs. more varied training data for a transparent nearest-example classifier | Two correct test labels out of four vs. four out of four on the *same four fictional test cases* | Dataset coverage, spurious feature shortcuts, proper limits of claims |
| **G8 B2**: Who shapes what we see? | Engagement-first vs. evidence/diversity-first ranking objective | The same five invented community posts appear in different orders | Recommendations are designed systems; attention and representation are not neutral |
| **G9 A1**: Test the claims | Familiar-only vs. broader edge-case evaluation | Four out of four familiar cases vs. five out of eight combined fictional cases | Performance claims depend on test scope, error cost and representativeness |
| **G9 B1**: AI companions, confidence and control | Alternative reactions to an overly personal fictional chatbot | Explanations and safer boundaries change | Simulated empathy, privacy, dependence, human support and agency |
| **G10 A3**: Create, test, transfer | Human-only vs. automation-first vs. bounded blended design | Benefits, hazards and evaluation needs differ | Defensible tool choice, accessible design, stakeholder feedback and accountability |

**Design limitation:** None of these simulations predict the behaviour of a real deployed model, person or social-media system. They deliberately make simplified mechanisms visible and should be followed by questions about real-world uncertainty. The G7 classifier implements a real transparent weighted nearest-example rule on a toy dataset, rather than presenting canned accuracy figures as if a complex neural network had been trained.

Students must explore **at least two different laboratory conditions** before they can complete the associated selected-response check. The interface clearly labels this as an investigation condition, not proof of mastery or real-world competence.

## Authentic design and evaluation

Four existing modules receive more explicit project scaffolds:

- **G8 A3** — inclusive user needs, prototype, usability test, feedback and revision.
- **G9 A3** — authentic public communication, source verification, representation, accessibility, consent and attribution.
- **G10 A3** — capstone with an authentic or teacher-provided stakeholder, alternatives, prototype, failure cases, privacy/accessibility testing, feedback, revision and defence.
- **G10 B3** — governance charter with stakeholder consultation, accountability, appeal and correction procedures, tested against a fictional failure case.

The browser records only existing local progress states. Written project notes, laboratory selections and teacher assessment do not become centralised records. Downloaded notes now include the specific investigation question, method, evidence expectation and applicable capstone checklist so a teacher can see the intended performance without accessing a pupil's local browser state.

## Original learning benchmarks

The redesign was *informed by* publicly described features of:

1. **Day of AI / MIT RAISE** — grade-aware learning progression, technical inquiry, authentic applications: https://dayofai.org/
2. **Experience AI (Raspberry Pi Foundation / Google DeepMind)** — training data, classification, bias and structured teaching resources: https://experience-ai.org/
3. **OECD / European Commission AI Literacy Framework** — engaging with, creating, managing and shaping AI: https://ailiteracyframework.org/
4. **UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students** — human-centred mindset, ethics, techniques and AI-system design: https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students
5. **Common Sense Education** — student-relevant digital life, chatbot boundaries, wellbeing and media literacy: https://www.commonsense.org/education/
6. **AI4K12** — progression in perception, representation, learning, interaction and societal impact: https://ai4k12.org/

These are benchmark perspectives rather than certificates of alignment. All released investigation and case text is original. We have not reproduced copyright-protected lesson packages or claimed endorsement.

## Teaching and assessment: the threshold for real learning

The five in-site stages are **Learn → Model → Practise → Apply → Reflect**. Use the following assessment logic:

| Evidence | What the website can check | What requires learner work or educator review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading and knowledge checks | Selected option correctness against an authored key | Whether an explanation is robust and transfer-ready |
| Laboratory comparisons | Which fictional conditions were viewed; model outputs | Whether the learner can explain cause, uncertainty and limitations |
| Guided challenge | A category, sequence or selected-choice key | Quality of the learner's justification, discussion or counterexample |
| Investigation | Self-declared completion and local notes | Real artefacts, source trails, methods, audience response and revision quality |
| Reflection | Presence of a written answer or confirmation of offline work | Honest learning evidence, independence and performance in an unfamiliar context |

Do **not** treat a completed module as certified proficiency. Teacher-reviewed evidence should distinguish observation from inference, assess the quality of reasoning, and accept well-defended AI **non-use** as a legitimate demonstration of judgement.

### Suggested assessment descriptors (age-relative)

- **Beginning:** recognises a familiar example or risk with substantial scaffolding.
- **Developing:** describes a mechanism, an evidence source and a justified step in a familiar situation.
- **Applying:** independently compares alternatives, tests against criteria and revises work in an authentic setting.
- **Transferring:** adapts the method to an unfamiliar issue, names uncertainty, includes different perspectives and defends a proportionate decision.

Do not equate these descriptors with grades: a Grade 10 learner may need scaffolded instruction on a new system, while a Grade 6 learner may show excellent transfer on a familiar one.

## The next human-quality gate

Before claiming an outstanding learning experience, run a moderated school-approved pilot across Grades 6–10. Include diverse language and accessibility needs. Ask learners to explain, unaided, why a model failed, how ranking changed information exposure, or what a trustworthy test would require. Use transfer questions unseen in the lesson, teachers' independent judgements of artefacts and evidence of actual revision. Review safety and cognitive load, especially the fictional AI-companion case. A polished page or passing CI cannot supply this evidence.
