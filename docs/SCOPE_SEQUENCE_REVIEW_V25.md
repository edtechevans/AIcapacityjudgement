# Capacity × Judgement · Scope-and-Sequence Review and Rebuild v25

**Grades:** 6–12 · **Strands:** six, unchanged · **Core modules:** 42 · **Interactive laboratories:** 11 · **Integrated grade-level performance challenges:** seven.

**Review status:** A structured, expert-informed desk critique using eLearning, assessment-design and AI-education criteria. No named independent expert has been recruited, interviewed, consulted or represented as an actual tester. The revised curriculum is **not** certified, officially mapped or endorsed by UNESCO, OECD, the European Commission, CSTA, AI4K12 or the IB, nor does automation verify student learning outcomes.

## Executive finding

The dual framework—**AI Capacity** (understanding, working, creating) and **AI Judgement** (purpose, trust, consequences)—is educationally coherent and unusually serious about learner agency, privacy, independent evidence, responsible non-use and authentic transfer.

Its weakness was not a lack of modules. Its *vertical curriculum* was hard to see and sometimes more advanced in ethical critique than in knowledge of **how AI systems represent information, learn from data and operate within complex designs**. Short in-site checks assessed guided decisions, while the quality of independent cross-strand evidence was not always explicit.

The redesign preserves the product's seven grade-specific experiences and five learner-controlled learning stages, and makes increasingly demanding intellectual moves and **independently reviewable performance tasks** visible. Grades 11–12 remain DP-oriented, independent and stakeholder-accountable, not simply harder MYP quizzes.

## Review lenses and resulting changes

| eLearning / AI-education lens | Diagnosis | Implemented response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Constructive alignment** | Strand titles remained stable, but the observable progression and intended evidence between years were implicit | 42 explicit grade × strand progress moves, each with a targeted misconception; visible on the lesson's first learning stage |
| **AI conceptual accuracy** | Critical-tool use sometimes outpaced explicit instruction on sensing, representation, training/testing and model-versus-product boundaries | Deeper A1 concept teaching across seven grades and a new Grade 8 representation laboratory with transparent deterministic rules |
| **Assessment for transfer** | Selected-response checks risk being mistaken for transferable understanding | Seven interdisciplinary, grade-specific performance tasks, requiring original work, test evidence, critique, revision and transfer to an unfamiliar context |
| **Authentic agency** | The difference between tool fluency and independent intellectual ownership needed reinforcing | Every annual challenge includes an AI/non-AI decision, independent source/evidence step and legitimate no-deployment option |
| **Responsible AI scope** | Ethics could be repeated as abstract 'be responsible' advice without increasing substance | A progression from consent/privacy → ownership and permissions → environmental/resources and inclusive design → consequential harms → governance, appeal and stewardship |
| **Cognitive load and motivation** | Adding a separate full course or prerequisite ladder would create unnecessary duplication | Same 42 modules, five teaching stages and grade picker; optional progressive-disclosure bridges, scope explorer and one integrated challenge at the end of each grade's course library |
| **Inclusive practice** | Learners need pathways that do not assume commercial AI accounts, native-English fluency, or keyboard/mouse parity | Fictional offline lab, accessible keyboard-focusable tables, paper and oral alternatives, real work retained in approved school systems |
| **Teacher confidence** | Educators could see activities without an explicit seven-year instructional destination | A complete inspectable progression map and annual performance criteria with planned peer/teacher moderation |

## Revised grade-level scope and sequence

| Grade | Developmental intellectual move | Evidence that matters | New or strengthened content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **6 · Explore & Question** | Explain inputs, processes, outputs and limits; distinguish fixed rules from learned patterns | Accurate conceptual explanation, two independent source checks, private-by-design creative artefact | Digital sensing and representation; reasoned non-use |
| **7 · Apply & Verify** | Distinguish training from unseen tests and follow information and creative contributions to their source | Controlled classifier comparison, source trail and permission-aware revised communication | Held-out testing, shortcuts, consent, image/voice/copyright permissions |
| **8 · Design & Decide** | Demonstrate how chosen features, modalities and ranking criteria shape results | Feature-representation comparison, ranking investigation and accessible user-tested prototype | **New experimental representation lab**; multimodal limitations, resource-aware inclusive design |
| **9 · Evaluate & Challenge** | Evaluate error consequences, synthetic-media provenance and persuasive interaction | Reproducible failure cases, provenance map and a safe human-controlled communication route | Model versus product claims, representational harms, privacy and environmental burdens |
| **10 · Transfer & Lead** | Evaluate adoption claims independently and defend a stakeholder-accountable decision | Comparative evaluation, test log, safeguards and oral defence | False positive/negative reasoning, subgroup analysis and transparent non-deployment conditions |
| **11 · Investigate & Architect** | Explain model/retrieval/tool/human system boundaries and build reproducible, constrained evaluations | Architecture and model cards, provenance audit and tested design dossier | System-level reliability, independent research, fair evaluation, permissions and impact |
| **12 · Create & Steward** | Demonstrate original, revised impact and continuing responsibility | Stakeholder-aware impact portfolio, incident protocol and oral/public defence | Multimodal/agentic systems, accountable orchestration, resource burdens and redress |

### How the six strands now spiral

**A1 — Understanding AI:** fixed rules and learned patterns → labelled training and testing → representation and multimodal inputs → failure under unfamiliar conditions → evaluation and error costs → system architecture → frontier capabilities and limits.

**A2 — Working with AI:** purposeful instructions → independent source verification → gated workflows → total effort and error recovery → bounded tool-selection decisions → constrained orchestration → governed autonomous and human-led alternatives.

**A3 — Creating with AI:** independently generated ideas → permission-aware revision → inclusive user testing → representation-aware design → defensible authentic prototypes → stakeholder-validated systems → original accountable impact.

**B1 — Purpose and Agency:** learning skill preservation → independent-first practice → metacognitive delegation → persuasive/relational limits → defended use/non-use → intellectual authorship → lifelong professional agency.

**B2 — Truth and Trust:** claim/source distinction → citation and source origin → ranking/representation effects → synthetic-media verification → conflicting evidence → provenance and independence → defended and revised inference.

**B3 — Ethics and Consequences:** privacy and consent → licensing/attribution and voice likeness → inclusion and environmental trade-offs → consequential harms → accountability and human appeal → fairness and service maintenance → incident response, correction and stewardship.

## Seven independently reviewable grade challenges

The website's **My courses** page for each grade now includes an integrated performance brief. These are not seven new graded modules; the intention is to **transfer** learning between strands and to expose misunderstandings a multiple-choice check cannot establish.

| Grade | Challenge | Evidence products |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | Trustworthy garden information card | Input–process–output sketch, sources, original card, privacy choice and revision |
| 7 | School club information assistant audit | Toy training/test comparison, provenance check, consent and accessible announcement |
| 8 | Inclusive information-feed redesign | Representation comparison, ranking choices, accessibility review and prototype revision |
| 9 | Disputed community alert investigation | Media provenance, consequential error tests, affected people and approved correction plan |
| 10 | Independent AI adoption review | Reproducible evaluation, alternatives, impact criteria, stop rules and oral defence |
| 11 | Accountable community service architecture | Systems diagram, model/system card, source audit, bounded prototype and review |
| 12 | Capacity × Judgement impact portfolio | Original intervention or non-AI proposal, evidence, feedback, revisions, oral defence and governance |

Each performance task publishes three success criteria, an independent feedback/revision prompt and an unfamiliar transfer question. Do not represent marked site practice as a teacher-rated attainment, nor grade students automatically from textual length or polished visual output.

## One new Grade 8 mechanism laboratory

**8-A1 · What did the system actually notice?**

A simple transparent fictional *rule-based* labeler reads four written leaf descriptions. A **colour-only** representation matches two of four invented labels; a **texture** representation matches four of four. When texture is hidden in two cases, the output is **insufficient information**, rather than an overconfident label.

This laboratory creates a conceptual bridge between Grade 7's skewed training-data classifier and Grade 9's reliability testing. It demonstrates that a *representation* captures only selected details; a trained model is not necessarily involved, and four cases cannot establish general performance. Learners must compare at least two conditions before passing the guided practice stage. The original Grade 8 model-comparison challenge remains intact.

## Framework comparison and attribution

This is a **directional comparison**, not formal equivalence to another organisation's competency statements.

- **UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students (2024; page updated 2026):** four dimensions (human-centred mindset; ethics; AI techniques/applications; AI system design), through Understand → Apply → Create. The new grade-level performance progression explicitly moves from conceptual explanation to independent application and accountable creation. https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students
- **OECD/European Commission, *Empowering Learners for the Age of AI* (18 June 2026):** Engage with, Create with, Manage and Shape AI. The six strands are not identical: collectively, they emphasise critical engagement, purposeful creation, independent management and the ability to question or shape systems. https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/empowering-learners-for-the-age-of-ai_65cd27d4-en.html
- **CSTA/AI4K12, *AI Learning Priorities for All K–12 Students* (2025):** emphasises representation/reasoning, machine learning, ethical systems, humans and AI, and societal impact. G8 representation and the cumulative A1 technical spine directly address concerns that AI education can neglect perception/representation and human interaction. https://csteachers.org/ai-priorities/
- **IB Diploma context:** TOK knowledge/evidence inquiry, original EE research and ethical CAS partnerships are *possible cross-curricular applications*, never substitute assessments or evidence of IB approval. Teachers must follow current subject and school AI-assistance policies.

## Moderation and evaluation still required

The next steps are **external or school-based**, and no implementation should claim they have already happened:

1. Review the full v25 strand/grade mapping with at least one AI/CS educator and one learning-design/assessment specialist, including misconceptions and degree of challenge.
2. Ask one or two subject educators at each grade band to attempt the integrated performance task, note real timing and identify inaccessible assumptions.
3. Pilot grade 8's representation lab on real devices, with keyboard and assistive-technology users; then run a formal WCAG-EM evaluation.
4. Gather independent student work across pre/post and unfamiliar transfer tasks, with trained human raters. Moderation should attend to subject knowledge, original authorship and decisions not to use AI.
5. Review DP1/DP2 workload with Diploma teachers to protect EE/TOK/CAS assessment integrity and avoid overloading students.

**Release validation is distinct from educational effectiveness.** GitHub Actions can demonstrate code/build test success; it cannot certify curriculum quality, formal accessibility conformance, external endorsement or improved student learning.
