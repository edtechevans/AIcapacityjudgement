# Learning Experience Review — Grades 6–10
**Date:** 14 November 2026  
**Scope:** all 30 AI Capacity × Judgement modules across Grades 6–10  
**Method:** critical expert-informed instructional design audit, code/content inspection and automated learner-journey checks. **No external experts were directly consulted and no student panel has yet tested this edition.**

## The standard: not a perfect quiz score, but better thinking

The Learning Studio is designed to help students **understand AI, make thoughtful choices, produce useful work, examine evidence and transfer their learning to new contexts**. Capacity and Judgement must remain equally visible, and a reasoned decision not to use AI must be a valid outcome.

An exceptional product is not merely engaging or beautiful. Students must learn something they didn't know, receive actionable feedback, apply it to a meaningful challenge and be able to explain *how their thinking changed*.

## Six specialist review lenses

| Lens | Critical finding | Implemented response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Instructional design / constructive alignment** | The core modules had objectives but their practical success indicators were not consistently visible before teaching and application. | Each of the 30 modules now opens with **three tailored “By the end, I can…” criteria**; the same indicators reappear before authentic application. |
| **Cognitive science / metacognition** | The worked example was informative but too easy to reveal passively, and prior-grade ideas weren't deliberately recalled. | Each module gets a specific **“Look for the difference”** prompt before revealing the stronger approach. Previously completed learning in the same strand can trigger a short **optional retrieval** prompt in the next grade. |
| **Formative assessment and feedback** | Wrong answers prompted repetition, but instructional next steps were overly general. | Incorrect understanding checks now offer **module-specific support**, and guided practice exposes optional hints and stretch challenges. The site does not automatically score the quality of open-response writing. |
| **UDL / inclusive learning design** | Students could select spoken or visual work but had to type the same amount of text as students selecting written work. | Students retain **written, spoken and visual options**, with shorter explanatory notes for non-written work and a choice of **written, spoken or sketched reflection**. Read-aloud remains available in the teaching sequence. |
| **Authentic application / transfer** | Application tasks had meaningful contexts but didn't consistently show clear standards or invite transfer outside the course. | Every module has **three content-specific success criteria**, a tailored cross-curricular transfer prompt, and extension work that can be completed with approved AI or without AI. |
| **Reflective assessment / learner agency** | A module could be marked complete immediately when the application form was submitted; the final reflection stage was passive. | **Application and reflection are now two separate checkpoints.** A module counts as practised only after the learner reflects on changed understanding and a future use, in an accessible response mode. This remains self-reported practice, not certified mastery. |

## What students now experience

1. **Learn:** Read ideas in small sections, see clear success indicators, access a glossary and optional read-aloud. Returning students can make a connection with prior learning.
2. **Model:** Look at a scenario and predict what matters in comparing weak/strong approaches, then reveal a model with a targeted challenge.
3. **Practise:** Choose, classify or sequence examples, articulate reasoning, review explanatory feedback, and open a hint if needed. Stretch tasks are optional.
4. **Apply:** Select an authentic challenge, choose how to express work, make an explicit AI-use or non-use decision, demonstrate evidence, revise and use the learning criteria as a quality guide.
5. **Reflect:** Explain what changed or what was learned, and identify a new application. Learners may write, discuss or sketch; writing is **not stored** by the site. Only after this step is the module recorded as practised.

## Curriculum continuity

The vertical scope follows G6 **Explore & Question**, G7 **Apply & Verify**, G8 **Design & Decide**, G9 **Evaluate & Challenge**, and G10 **Transfer & Lead**.

Each grade contains three **Capacity** strands (Understanding AI, Working with AI, Creating with AI) and three **Judgement** strands (Purpose & Agency, Truth & Trust, Ethics & Consequences). The new scaffold files are individually authored for each of the 30 grade–strand combinations, not recycled labels. Previously completed strands can cue a non-graded recall task in the next grade.

## Research informing this review

- [UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students (2024)](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students): human-centred mindset, ethics, AI techniques and applications, and system design; progression from understanding to applying and creating.
- [CAST Universal Design for Learning Guidelines 3.0](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/): flexible representation, engagement, communication and expression, strategic learning, learner agency and transfer.
- [EEF Metacognition and Self-Regulation](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/metacognition-and-self-regulation/): explicit modelling, planning, monitoring, evaluating and applying strategies within curriculum content.
- [EEF Teacher Feedback to Improve Pupil Learning](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/feedback): effective instruction comes first; feedback needs to support actionable improvement.

These are **sources used for design principles**, not evidence that UNESCO, CAST or EEF endorsed or audited this website.

## Evidence of functioning

- Content tests cover all 30 modules, scaffolds and instructional sequence, from first concept to final reflection.
- Browser checks validate desktop/tablet/phone/small-phone, deep links, progress, actual note export, primary accessibility issues and complete lesson journeys.
- New tests explicitly prove that **submitting the application does not count as completed reflection**.
- Persistent data continues to hold only local checkpoints and check-in selections; written artefacts remain in the open tab unless the student exports them.

## What's still required to substantiate “10/10”

**Real learner validation:** In a moderated pilot, involve two students per grade. Test the ability to understand new material, make a justified AI-use/non-use decision, apply it in a subject challenge, receive or seek feedback, reflect and transfer.

**Human review:** Have a qualified subject educator and accessibility reviewer independently inspect a spread of grade-appropriate modules, including factual accuracy, safety/permissions, language demand, cultural responsiveness and authentic task feasibility.

**Authentic learning evidence:** The app does not mark written output for accuracy or reasoning quality, verify external artefacts, capture teacher feedback, provide student accounts or demonstrate retained learning. These require a wider architecture and safeguarding plan if used operationally.

A perfect automated test result is **not** a perfect learning result. This revision makes the instruction more intentional and assessable while avoiding claims the software cannot support.
